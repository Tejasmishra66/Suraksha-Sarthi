<p>SDRF HELPING HANDS - Quick Summary</p><h1>Problem 1: Slow Communication Between Departments</h1><ul><li><strong>Digital Task Board:</strong> A simple screen where commanders assign tasks directly to specific departments.</li><li><strong>Smart Location Alerts:</strong> The system automatically messages only the officials in charge of the disaster area.</li></ul><h1>Problem 2: Messy and Fake Field Data</h1><ul><li><strong>Offline Mode:</strong> The app saves reports when there's no internet and uploads them automatically later.</li><li><strong>Easy Forms:</strong> Users pick from drop-down menus instead of typing, reducing mistakes.</li><li><strong>Fake-Proof Photos:</strong> Photos must be taken inside the app (no gallery uploads) and are locked with exact GPS and time.</li></ul><h1>Problem 3: Managing Volunteers &amp; Resources</h1><ul><li><strong>Skill Tags:</strong> Volunteers are labeled by their specific skills (e.g., Mountain Rescue) to send the right people.</li><li><strong>Local Alerts:</strong> Only volunteers within a 10km radius get notified to avoid confusion.</li><li><strong>Offline Guides:</strong> The app auto-downloads survival and rescue manuals to use without internet.</li></ul><h1>Problem 4: Departments Missing Important Information</h1><ul><li><strong>Official News Feed:</strong> A verified, category-based timeline just for government updates (like a secure Twitter).</li><li><strong>Map Pins:</strong> Agencies can drop warning pins on a shared map so everyone sees the danger instantly.</li></ul><h1>Problem 5: Knowing if the App/System is Down</h1><ul><li><strong>Auto-Check:</strong> The app silently checks in every 5 minutes. If it stops, the dashboard warns the commander.</li><li><strong>SMS Backup:</strong> If the app is offline, the system automatically sends an emergency SMS or phone call to the officer.</li></ul><h1>Problem 6: Tracking Equipment (Asset Management)</h1><ul><li><strong>QR Code Tracking:</strong> Every important tool has a QR code. Scan it when it leaves, scan again when it returns.</li><li><strong>Transfer System:</strong> If moving tools to another office, the sender scans 'Dispatch' and the receiver scans 'Confirm'.</li><li><strong>Live Equipment Map:</strong> A web screen showing exactly where all equipment is right now, based on its last scan.</li></ul><h1>System Architecture: The Best Database</h1><ul><li><strong>Top Choice - PostgreSQL (with PostGIS):</strong> It is the absolute best for this project. It handles location/map data perfectly, manages heavy emergency traffic without crashing, and stores offline sync data easily.</li><li><strong>Why not MongoDB?:</strong> It struggles to link related data together (like assigning a specific tool to a specific office).</li><li><strong>Why not MySQL?:</strong> Its map and location-tracking features are very weak compared to PostgreSQL.</li><li><strong>Why not SQLite?:</strong> It is great for the mobile app's offline storage, but the main web server will crash if 50 disaster teams try to upload data at the exact same second.</li><li><strong>Why not Firebase?:</strong> It makes complex searches very difficult (e.g., finding volunteers with specific skills who are also within a 10km radius).</li></ul>
